# Volkswagen Jetta (Mk5 Generation, 2005-2010)

## Overview
The fifth-generation Jetta was a compact sedan (with a SportWagen wagon
variant added later in the generation) that leaned into a more upscale,
European-influenced design and interior quality than its predecessor,
positioning it slightly above mainstream compact-sedan competitors like the
Corolla or Civic of the same era.

## Trims
Common trim levels across this generation: Value Edition, S, SE, SEL, and
two performance/efficiency-focused variants -- the GLI (sport-tuned) and TDI
(diesel).

## Engines and Drivetrain
- Base engine: 2.5L inline-5, roughly 150 hp, paired with a 5-speed manual
  or 6-speed automatic (Tiptronic).
- GLI trim: 2.0L turbocharged four-cylinder, roughly 200 hp, available with
  manual or DSG dual-clutch automatic.
- TDI trim: 1.9L (early years) or 2.0L (later years) turbo-diesel
  four-cylinder, notably fuel-efficient for the segment.
- All-wheel drive was not offered on this generation; front-wheel drive only.

## Fuel Economy
TDI diesel models were the standout here, commonly rated in the
low-to-mid 30s mpg combined, with highway figures reported into the low 40s
by owners. Gas-engine models (2.5L, 2.0T) typically ranged from the low 20s
to high 20s mpg combined.

## Safety
Front airbags were standard across the generation; side and curtain airbags
became standard equipment partway through the generation rather than from
the first model year. Anti-lock brakes (ABS) and electronic stability
control were standard on most trims by the later years of this generation.

## Known Reliability Notes
Owners of early model years in this generation have commonly reported
ignition coil pack failures on the 2.5L engine, and window regulator
mechanism failures (windows dropping into the door) are a frequently cited
complaint across the generation. These are commonly reported issues, not
confirmed defects across every unit -- treat as a pattern to be aware of
when evaluating a specific used example, not a certainty.
